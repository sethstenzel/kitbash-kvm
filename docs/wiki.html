KitBash KVM

A browser and desktop application for controlling a remote computer using a USB capture card and a CH9329-based HID adapter. Stream live video and forward keyboard and mouse input — no software needed on the target machine.

Overview

KitBash KVM works by combining two hardware components:

  • USB Capture Card — captures the target machine's video and audio output and presents it as a webcam to your browser.
  • CH9329 / NanoKVM USB Adapter — receives serial commands from KitBash KVM and emulates a USB keyboard and mouse on the target machine.

Together these let you see and control any machine that has a USB port and a video output, including machines during BIOS, OS installation, or without a network connection.

  • Web app: controller.kitbashkvm.com in a Chromium-based browser (Chrome, Edge) — Web Serial is required for keyboard and mouse control. The web app can be installed as an app (PWA) and updates itself automatically.
  • Desktop app: the same app built with Tauri, with an extra "keep window on top" button.

Getting Started

  1. Connect your USB capture card to the target machine's video output (HDMI, DP, VGA via adapter).
  2. Connect your CH9329 / NanoKVM USB adapter between your computer and a USB port on the target machine.
  3. Open KitBash KVM in your browser or launch the desktop app, and allow camera and microphone access when asked. (If access was denied, the source icons show a warning badge — click one to ask again.)
  4. Click the Video Source icon in the command bar and pick your capture card — the stream starts right away. Optionally pick the capture card's audio from the Audio Source icon.
  5. Set the correct Baud Rate in Settings → Settings tab.
  6. Click Connect KVM (Serial) and choose the adapter's port in the browser's port chooser. If no baud rate has been set yet, the app asks for one (default 9600).
  7. Keyboard input is now forwarded to the target. Click the video to capture the mouse.
💡
Use baud rate 9600 for a KitBash KVM or 57600 for a Sipeed USB KVM (set automatically when a KitBash capture card is detected).
ℹ
The selected video device is remembered: it starts automatically next time, and if it is not plugged in yet, the stream starts as soon as it appears.
KitBash capture cards are picked automatically. On start, the app looks for a KitBash capture card and, if one is plugged in, selects it (overriding the last-used devices), sets the matching Baud Rate, and starts streaming:
PreferenceVideo deviceAudio deviceBaud rate

Names are matched by "contains", so Chrome's Default - Digital Audio Interface (…) entry matches too; the card's own audio entry is preferred over that alias so the selection doesn't follow the Windows default device.

Icon Colours

  • Video Source / Audio Source icons turn green while that source is live.
  • Stop Media Devices and Reset Media Devices are red.
  • Record turns red and blinks while recording.
  • Screenshot and Record are greyed out, and OCR / QR are hidden, while no video is streaming.

Docked vs. Floating Command Bar

Choose in Settings → Video tab → Command Bar:

ModeBehaviour
Floating Command Bar (default)A rounded bar that slides down over the video. It hides while you control the target and comes back when you release control.
Docked Command BarA full-width strip that is always visible above the video, with the icons centred.

Auto-Hide Behaviour (Floating)

  • Typing on the target or moving the mouse in the captured video area hides the bar.
  • Holding Esc for about a second releases keyboard & mouse and shows the bar again (see Keyboard Capture). In relative mouse mode, leaving pointer lock also shows it.
  • Starting a QR or OCR scan hides the bar until the scan ends.
  • With KVM input disabled, the bar shows when you move the mouse and hides (together with the cursor) after 3 seconds of inactivity.

Dragging

Click and drag on any open area of the command bar to reposition it horizontally (up to 25% of the screen width in either direction).

Video Only Mode

The Video Only Mode button hides the command bar and stops forwarding keyboard and mouse input, so you can simply watch the target. Press Esc to exit.

When the stream starts, a notification shows the device name, resolution, and frame rate.

If the capture card is unplugged, the stream stops with a warning and resumes automatically when it is plugged back in. A missing or busy audio device never blocks video: the stream starts video-only and audio is added back when the device returns.

Resolution & Frame Rate

By default the stream runs in Auto mode: the app requests 1920×1080 @ 60 fps and the capture card delivers the best mode it supports up to that (for example 1080p30 on MS2109-based cards, 1080p60 on MS2130).

The first time a device streams, the app tests which resolution/FPS combinations it really supports and caches the list per device. To pick a specific mode:

  1. Start the video stream.
  2. Open Settings → Video tab.
  3. Select a mode from the Resolution & Frame Rate dropdown (or Auto (best available, up to 1080p @ 60fps)).
  4. Click Apply Resolution & Frame Rate Settings — the stream restarts in that mode and an overlay shows the actual and requested resolution for a few seconds.
ℹ
The chosen mode is remembered for that device. Click Recheck Video Settings to clear the cached list and re-test the device.

Flip & Rotate

Found in Settings → Video tab → Flip & Rotate Video:

  • Rotate: None, 90°, −90°, 180°
  • Flip: Mirror (left-right) and Flip upside down (can be combined)

Mouse input, screenshots, OCR and QR scans all follow the rotation and flips. Reset display settings (under the resolution controls) puts rotation, flips and fit mode back to their defaults.

KVM Input (Keyboard & Mouse)

Connecting

  1. Choose the correct Baud Rate in Settings → Settings tab:
Baud RateDevice
9600KitBash KVM default (standard CH9329 firmware)
57600Sipeed USB KVM default
115200Other (e.g. modified CH9329 firmware)
  1. Click Connect KVM (Serial) in the command bar and select the adapter's port.

After connecting, the app asks the chip for its info. If it does not answer, a warning says the baud rate may be wrong. Changing the baud rate while connected closes the connection; connect again afterwards.

⚠
If the adapter is unplugged or the connection fails, all keys and buttons are released, a KVM serial device disconnected warning appears and the connect button returns. Click Disconnect KVM (Serial) to close the connection yourself.

Keyboard Capture

Once connected, keystrokes are forwarded to the target machine. Clicking the video also captures the mouse.

  • Keys are sent by physical position (like a real USB keyboard), so the target's keyboard layout decides which character appears. Full modifier support: Ctrl Shift Alt AltGr Win/Cmd
  • Esc is forwarded to the target like any other key.
  • Hold Esc for about 1 second to release keyboard & mouse capture — the command bar reappears. Click the video to capture again.
  • While the mouse is over the command bar, or one of its menus is open, key presses are not sent to the target (so e.g. Shift+Click on a toolbar button works). Open dialogs and Settings also keep their keys.
  • All held keys and buttons are released on the target when the window loses focus.
  • Keyboard Lock: in browser fullscreen while the KVM is connected, shortcuts such as Ctrl+W, Ctrl+T, Ctrl+N and Alt+Tab go to the target instead of your browser/OS (Chromium browsers).
💡
Keyboard Compatible Mode: Enable in Settings if keystrokes behave incorrectly on Linux or older systems. Keys are tapped (pressed and released at once) instead of held; this breaks Ctrl+scroll-wheel zoom.

Pasting the Clipboard (Ctrl+V)

With Type clipboard on Ctrl+V enabled (default), pressing Ctrl+V (Cmd+V on Mac) while controlling the target types your local clipboard text on the target. If the clipboard holds no text, a normal Ctrl+V is sent. Ctrl+Shift+V is always sent as a normal keystroke. Only ASCII characters can be typed.

Mouse Modes

Set in Settings → Settings tab → Cursor Mode.

Absolute Mode (default)

Mouse position maps directly to coordinates on the target screen.

  • Absolute Mouse Area Width / Height: Configure what percentage of the video maps to the target screen (10–100%, centred). Useful when the target resolution doesn't fill the entire capture area. While Settings is open, the area is outlined in red.

Relative Mode

Uses pointer lock — movements are captured as deltas and forwarded directly. Recommended for Linux and Android targets, which may not support absolute positioning.

Auto Capture Mouse

When enabled (default), clicking the video immediately enters capture mode. When disabled, a confirmation dialog appears first.

Send Text

Sends a string of plain text to the target machine as a series of keystrokes.

  1. Open the Text Transfer menu in the command bar and choose Send Text....
  2. Type or paste your text in the dialog.
  3. Press OK or Ctrl+Enter to send.
  • Characters are sent one at a time with a small inter-key delay. Line breaks are sent as Enter, tabs as Tab.
  • Only ASCII characters are supported. Unsupported characters are skipped and listed in a warning notification.
  • Uppercase letters are handled automatically using Shift.
  • While text is being sent, a Stop Sending Text button appears in the command bar. Click it to cancel the current send and anything queued behind it. Text, hotkeys, clipboard pastes and macros share one queue, so they never mix.
💡
For text with special keys or delays, use Send Advanced Text instead.

Send Advanced Text

Sends text with embedded command sequences for pressing special keys, modifier combinations, and timed delays — useful for automating login sequences, scripts, and repetitive tasks.

  1. Open the Text Transfer menu and choose Send Adv. Text (Command Key Support)....
  2. Enter your text and commands using the escape sequence syntax below.
  3. Press OK or Ctrl+Enter to send.

Macros

Macros let you save frequently used advanced text sequences and run them instantly from the command bar. Macros can be grouped into categories.

Creating a Macro

  1. Open Settings → Macros tab.
  2. Click Add Macro.
  3. Enter a name (up to 30 characters), choose a Category (defaults to the category you're viewing, otherwise Default) and enter the macro content using Advanced Text syntax.
  4. Click Save.

New macros start in the category currently selected in the category filter.

Categories

Every macro belongs to a category. The built-in Default category always exists, holds new macros unless you pick another category, and can't be renamed or deleted. Macros saved by older versions are moved into it automatically.

ActionHow
CreateClick Add Category in Macro Management and enter a name (up to 30 characters, must be unique)
RenameClick the pencil, edit the name, then press Enter or click the check mark (Esc cancels)
DeleteClick the trash icon — its macros move to Default (confirmation required if it contains macros)

Running a Macro

Once the KVM is connected, a Macros menu appears in the command bar. Categories are listed as parent items (with a macro count) that open a sub-menu. Empty categories are not shown, and when only one category has macros they are listed directly. Select a macro to run it immediately using the same engine as Send Advanced Text.

Managing Macros

ActionDescription
Edit ✏Modify the macro name, category or message
Delete 🗑Remove a single macro
Clear AllDelete all macros (requires confirmation; categories are kept)
ImportLoad macros and categories from a .json file
ExportSave all macros and categories to a timestamped .json file

Exported files are named kbkvm_macros_YYYY-MM-DD_HH-mm-ss.json and include the categories.

⚠
Importing replaces the current macros and categories. Macros from files exported by older versions (without categories) go into Default; an exported category named Default is merged into it. Invalid entries are skipped and reported.

Searching Macros

Use the search box in the Macros tab to filter macros by name or message content in real time. The category filter below it (All categories or a single category — each with a count) narrows the list first, and the search applies within it.

QR Code Scanner

Scan QR codes displayed on the target machine's screen without needing to type them manually.

  1. Click the QR Code Scanner button in the command bar (shown while video is streaming).
  2. A darkened selection overlay appears over the video stream.
  3. Drag to draw a box around the QR code — the selected area shows clearly while the rest dims.
  4. Release — the app decodes the QR code and copies the result to your clipboard.
  • A notification confirms success or indicates no code was found.
  • Press Escape to cancel without scanning.
  • The hint text fades out after 2 seconds so it doesn't obscure the image.
  • The scan uses the full native video resolution and follows rotation/flip settings.
💡
Auto-Open QR Code Links: Enable in Settings to open a scanned URL in a new browser tab. Browsers often block tabs opened after a scan, so the link is also shown in a notification to click.

OCR Text Extraction

Extract text visible on the target machine's screen using optical character recognition (Tesseract.js).

  1. Click the Text Extraction (OCR) button in the command bar (shown while video is streaming).
  2. A darkened selection overlay appears over the video stream.
  3. Drag to draw a box around the text — the selected area shows clearly while the rest dims.
  4. Release — the app runs OCR and copies the extracted text to your clipboard.
  • A notification confirms success or warns if no text was found.
  • Press Escape to cancel without scanning.
  • The selected area is read at the full native video resolution, upright according to the rotation/flip settings.
  • The OCR engine and English language data are downloaded on first use; processing may take a few seconds for large areas.
💡
Auto-Open OCR Extracted URLs: Enable in Settings to open the first URL found in the extracted text. Found URLs are also shown as clickable links in a notification.
ℹ
OCR works best on clear, high-contrast text at a reasonable font size. Increase the video resolution for better accuracy.

Video Recording

  1. Click the Record Video button to start recording (video and, if live, audio).
  2. The button turns red and blinks while recording is active.
  3. Click again to stop — the recording is saved after about a second and downloads automatically as record_YYYY-MM-DD_HH-mm-ss.webm (or .mp4 where WebM isn't supported).

If the video stream stops while recording, the recording is saved automatically.
